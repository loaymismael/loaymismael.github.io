Hospital Emergency Room Dashboard

A documented portfolio project exploring healthcare data and turning analysis into careful, actionable questions.

Project date: 2026Status: Completed
Open GitHub Repository ↗Back to All Projects

Business Problem

Hospital emergency departments generate operational data that can help teams understand patient flow, waiting time, admissions, and satisfaction. A dashboard brings these measures into one place for exploration.

Approach

Prepared the hospital emergency room dataset in Power Query, created a date table and supporting calculated fields, and developed an interactive Power BI report for exploring patient volume, admission status, demographics, referrals, wait time, and satisfaction.

Key Findings

  • The dashboard organizes emergency-room activity into a single interactive view.
  • Date, demographic, referral, and admission dimensions support more focused operational analysis.
  • Wait-time and satisfaction measures can be explored together to identify questions for further investigation.

Recommendations

  • Review waiting-time patterns alongside patient satisfaction and admission outcomes.
  • Use date and department filters to investigate operational variation before proposing process changes.
  • Validate findings with operational teams and additional context before making clinical or staffing decisions.

Business Impact

Creates a reusable visual starting point for operational review and stakeholder discussion. Any intervention or measured improvement would require separate evaluation.

Clinical Relevance

Supports conversations about patient flow and service experience; it is not a clinical decision-support system.

Technologies Used

Power BIPower QueryDAXData ModelingData Visualization

What I Learned

Designing a dashboard requires more than visuals: consistent definitions, useful filters, clear labels, and a layout aligned with the questions decision-makers need to answer.

Interpretation note: results describe the project dataset and its available fields. They should not be treated as causal evidence, individual clinical predictions, or proof of real-world intervention impact.
