Medical Appointment No-Shows Analysis

A documented portfolio project exploring healthcare data and turning analysis into careful, actionable questions.

Project date: 2026Status: Completed
Open GitHub Repository ↗Back to All Projects

Business Problem

Missed outpatient appointments can disrupt clinic schedules and reduce access to care. The project explores how appointment timing and patient or appointment characteristics relate to attendance in the selected dataset.

Approach

Used SQL Server to clean, enrich, and validate appointment records, then examined attendance patterns by lead time, same-day versus future appointments, age group, gender, scholarship status, selected health indicators, and neighbourhood.

Key Findings

  • Same-day appointments had a 4.65% no-show rate in the analyzed dataset, compared with 28.52% for future appointments.
  • The observed no-show rate rose across several lead-time groups, reaching 34.15% for appointments scheduled 31–60 days ahead.
  • Rates varied across age groups and neighbourhoods; these are descriptive associations, not proof of cause.

Recommendations

  • Investigate reminder and confirmation workflows for appointments with longer lead times.
  • Validate patterns against current local data before operationalizing any risk-based outreach.
  • Assess interventions prospectively and monitor fairness, privacy, and unintended effects.

Business Impact

Provides a structured, auditable SQL workflow and descriptive findings that can inform further investigation. No intervention impact is claimed.

Clinical Relevance

Attendance patterns may help healthcare teams formulate access and scheduling questions; the analysis does not diagnose patients or establish individual risk.

What I Learned

Data validation is essential before interpreting rates. Group definitions and denominator choices can materially change the story.

Interpretation note: results describe the project dataset and its available fields. They should not be treated as causal evidence, individual clinical predictions, or proof of real-world intervention impact.
